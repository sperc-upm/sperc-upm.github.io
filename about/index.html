About SPERC

A research centre in the Department of Chemical and Environmental Engineering, Faculty of Engineering, Universiti Putra Malaysia.

Centre description

SPERC takes a multidisciplinary, whole-systems approach to technologies and processes, considering environmental, economic, social and sustainability aspects. Its work includes green technology, alternative resources, and the design and operation of systems that use energy and resources more sustainably.

Strategic direction 2027–2030

Four research thrusts, each led by one group, and a shared platform that every project draws on.

SPERC Strategic Plan 2027–2030, section 2 (draft) As of 7 Oct 2026
  1. T1

    Biomass-to-Carbon and Carbon Removal

    Engineered biochar for soil, cement and carbon credits; CO2 capture sorbents from waste; carbon accounting (MRV) for palm and agro-waste.

    Led by Carbon Capture & Biomass Utilisation Research Group (CCBU)

  2. T2

    Hydrogen from Palm and Agro-Waste

    Biohydrogen from POME and food waste; gasification and reforming to H2; low-cost catalysts; hydrogen safety.

    Led by Hydrogen Nexus Research Group (HyNeX)

  3. T3

    Circular Bioeconomy and Biorefinery

    Microalgae biorefinery; enzymes and fermentation products; bio-based materials and natural-fibre composites.

    Led by Biochemical Engineering Advanced Research Group (BEAR)

  4. T4

    Water and Waste Resource Recovery

    Industrial wastewater and POME to resources (nutrients, water reuse); landfill leachate; plastics and e-waste; emerging contaminants.

    Led by Environmental Technology Research Group (EnTeCH)

  5. P

    Digital, Sustainability and Safety Science

    Every thrust project carries a techno-economic analysis, an LCA and, where data allow, a machine-learning model; process safety for the new hydrogen and bio-processes.

Towards HICoE status

SPERC is working towards recognition as a Higher Institution Centre of Excellence (HICoE) by the Ministry of Higher Education. It assesses its own record against the HICoE R&I Instrument (version 2.1, 2018) and uses the 2027–2030 strategic plan to close the gaps.
